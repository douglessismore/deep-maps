# Deep Maps — Content Audit for AI Council Review

> **What is Deep Maps?** An interactive geospatial storytelling platform. Users explore curated historical moments tied to real places on a map. Each "moment" is a specific event at a specific lat/lng. We have 602 moments, 195 stories, 210 entities, and 30 collections. The app renders on mobile + desktop with a Leaflet map.
>
> **Your role**: Review ALL content below for quality, consistency, tone, and accuracy. Flag issues. Suggest improvements. Be direct and specific.

---

## Your Audit Checklist

For each moment/story/entity you review, check:

1. **Five-Second Test**: Can someone who has never used the app understand what happened here within 5 seconds of reading the moment name + subtitle?
2. **Verb-First Event Name**: Is the moment name an event description, not a place name or noun phrase?
3. **Subtitle adds value**: Does the subtitle provide the "second hook" (irony, stakes, human detail) WITHOUT restating the name?
4. **Description is standalone**: Does the description work as a mini-article without requiring story context?
5. **Past tense consistency**: Are events described in past tense (preferred) or headline present ("Caesar Is Stabbed")? Flag any inconsistencies.
6. **Neutral encyclopedic tone**: Does the content maintain Wikipedia-style matter-of-fact clarity without being preachy, sensational, or devotional?
7. **Factual accuracy**: Anything that seems wrong, anachronistic, or unverifiable?
8. **Entity descriptions**: Do they frontload the hook in the first 8 words? (No "Born [real name]" openings.)

## SPECIAL REVIEW: Biblical & Sacred Content Tone

**This is a key question we need your input on.** Our ~71 sacred-history moments span Jesus's ministry (19 moments), Moses & the Exodus (9), Paul's Journeys (12), Abraham (7), King David (7), plus standalone biblical events and Catholic/Maya sacred history.

**The tension**: How to write about religious events with integrity that respects believers without alienating secular readers. We need to avoid both devotional language ("God performed a miracle") and dismissive hedging ("allegedly," "supposedly").

**Current approach**: We describe biblical events as narrative events at specific places, focusing on human drama and archaeological/physical detail. Examples:

**Moment name** (event-driven, no theological claims):
> "Jesus Is Crucified at Golgotha Outside the Walls of Jerusalem"

**Subtitle** (physical/human detail):
> "Where Roman soldiers nailed a Galilean preacher to a cross between two thieves"

**Description** (historical geography focus):
> References the Church of the Holy Sepulchre, archaeological debates, and the site's history.

**Potential issues to flag**:
- Some subtitles present supernatural events as fact: "an angel said 'He is not here; he has risen'" — should this use "according to the Gospels" framing?
- Story-level descriptions use literary narrative: "a sea that parted, a mountain wrapped in fire" — compelling but reads as accepted history
- The Guadalupe story claims "an image appears that scientists still cannot explain" — strong empirical claim that needs framing
- Non-Christian sacred content (Chichen Itza, Islamic sites, Eastern religions) may have different tone calibration

**Please recommend where on this spectrum each piece of content should fall:**
```
DEVOTIONAL ←————————————————————————→ SKEPTICAL
"God spoke"  "tradition holds"  "the Bible records"  "allegedly"
```
We're targeting the zone between "tradition holds" and "the Bible records" — descriptive, factual about the narrative, without asserting or denying theological truth claims.
